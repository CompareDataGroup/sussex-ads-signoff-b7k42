Before any of these run

  1. They are financial promotions. An ad that invites people to invest in an unlisted company has to be approved by an FCA-authorised firm that is allowed to approve promotions, unless an exemption applies.
  2. The usual exemptions do not fit a public ad. The high net worth and sophisticated investor routes rely on each person signing a statement first.
  3. Meta has its own rule. It only lets FCA-authorised advertisers run investment ads to UK audiences, so these would need to run through an authorised firm.
  4. The risk warning stays on. Every still carries the FCA wording. The caption also needs a link to a risk summary and the approving firm's name and number.
  5. Name the issuer. Confirm which company is issuing the shares and that its HMRC advance assurance covers this round.

This is not legal advice. Take these to your lawyers and to the firm that will approve them.
